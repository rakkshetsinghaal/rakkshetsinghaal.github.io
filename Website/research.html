  1. Judicial Memory Skepticism in Sexual-Assault Appellate Decisions: A Computational Analysis of Discourse Asymmetry Toward Vulnerable Adult Complainants in US State Appellate Opinions, 2000–2025

    Applied Cognitive Psychology · 2026

    Proposal accepted for special issue; manuscript under review

    Abstract

    Cognitive psychology holds that inconsistency within a witness's account is at best weakly diagnostic of whether that memory is accurate. This paper asks whether U.S. state appellate opinions in sexual-assault cases treat complainant memory in line with that consensus, and whether the answer differs by complainant vulnerability. Drawing on 59,340 published majority opinions from 110 state appellate courts (2000–2025), within-opinion regressions with court fixed effects show that opinions involving intoxicated complainants take a more skeptical stance toward memory (β = +0.033, p = .001), while those involving cognitively impaired complainants take a substantially less skeptical one (β = −0.167, p < .001). Dispositions do not differ by complainant category once court and year are accounted for. The intoxicated-complainant subdomain shows the largest gap between appellate discourse and the cognitive-science benchmark.

  2. Sowing Dependency: Unravelling the Rational Addiction of Agricultural Fertilizer Subsidies in India

    Agricultural Economics Research Review, 38(1), 61–70 · 2025

    Abstract

    By utilizing the rational addiction framework, this article investigates the dynamics of fertilizer consumption influenced by subsidy policies. By estimating a demand equation that incorporates addictive behaviours (tolerance, reinforcement and withdrawal), the research highlights how past consumption influences future usage, creating a cycle of dependency that is difficult to break. Empirical evidence supports the hypothesis that subsidy dependence in Indian agriculture mimics addictive behaviour, with farmers increasing consumption over time despite diminishing returns. This addiction not only perpetuates environmental and financial inefficiencies but also hinders policy reforms aimed at sustainable agricultural practices and showcases the need for a gradual transition towards more sustainable farming methods.

Additional Research

  1. Rational Inattention in Chess: Cognitive Precision, the Shadow Price of Deliberation Time, and Their Complementarity

    Working paper

    Abstract

    A poor decision made under the clock can arise from a lack of skill or a shortage of time, and the two leave much the same trace. Chess observes both unusually well, since every candidate move can be scored and the clock is recorded after each move. This paper estimates a rational inattention model on 36 million chess decisions by 243,595 players, separating a reflexive pull toward captures and checks from genuine skill and treating the clock as one budget spread across a whole game. One more second of clock is worth roughly three times as much to an elite player as to everyone else, and its value rises almost one for one with skill. A typical club player matches a master's expected result on an ordinary move by thinking about five times as long, while a player in the weakest one and a half percent would need closer to ten thousand times as long.

  2. Market Integration and the Direction of Weather-Shock Responses in African Smallholder Agriculture

    Working paper

    Abstract

    Standard treatments of weather shocks in smallholder agriculture model market integration as a scalar: better-connected households smooth shocks more effectively, but every margin is presumed to move in the same direction. This paper tests a sharper claim, that integration changes which direction specific margins move. Using LSMS-ISA household panels from seven African countries (96,994 household-wave observations, 2009–2022), a satellite-derived rainfall shock, and travel time to the nearest city as a proxy for integration, it estimates household fixed-effects regressions that interact the shock with remoteness. Under a shock, hired-in agricultural wages rise in integrated areas and fall in remote ones, local output prices move the opposite way, and off-farm hours shift away from remote areas (all p < 0.001). Nigeria and Niger, agroecologically similar but very differently integrated, match five of five and three of three regime predictions respectively.
