About

I am a Pre-Doctoral Fellow at the Global Poverty Research Lab at Northwestern University, working in development economics and political economy. I hold an M.A. in International and Development Economics from Yale University and a B.A. in Economics and Political Science from Alma College, and I was previously a Research Analyst at UC Berkeley's Haas School of Business.

One question runs through my work: who should decide how resources meant for the poor are used? Aid and public programs are often designed far from the communities they serve. My research asks what that distance costs, when moving decisions closer to recipients helps, and when local power gets in the way. More broadly, I am interested in how institutions and incentives shape the choices of households, farmers, and governments, and I study these questions with field experiments, causal machine learning, and structural models.
